01

Taking Sheldon — the checkpoints

Georgia does not require a rabies antibody titre test for dogs from at-risk countries like India, and imposes no quarantine when the rules are met — that's what makes it feasible for late November when most of Europe isn't (the EU's titre test alone adds a three-month wait). The only real timing gate is a valid rabies shot.

Step 1 · India

ISO microchip

15-digit ISO 11784/11785 chip, implanted before the rabies vaccination. Same number on every document.

Step 2 · India

Rabies vaccination — valid before travel

Georgia requires the primary rabies vaccine at least 30 days before arrival. For a late-Nov trip, have his rabies shot completed by about 23 October 2026.

the one date that governs everything
Step 3 · India

Parasite treatment

Deworming + tick/flea treatment before travel (sensible after the tick-fever); note it on his record.

Step 4 · India

AQCS Bangalore export certificate + NOC

Book AQCS ~1 week ahead; present Sheldon ~7 days before departure. Export certificate valid only 10 days. Keep the originals — they let you re-import him without quarantine.

Step 5 · Tbilisi

Arrival in Georgia

Entry via Tbilisi airport; National Food Agency may check documents + microchip. No titre, no quarantine when compliant.

Step 6 · Return

Bringing Sheldon home to India

Apply for an Advance NOC from AQCS ≥7 working days before flying back, under the Re-import category (Customs Circular 25/2013), using your original export docs — avoids the 15-day quarantine on fresh imports. Carry a Georgian vet health certificate dated close to departure.

03

Getting there — the flights that actually take Sheldon

The biggest surprise in this whole plan: the nonstop doesn't work. IndiGo is the only airline flying nonstop from India to Tbilisi, and it carries no pets on international routes at all — so the route that looks best on a booking site is the one Sheldon can't be on. What you actually want is one airline, end to end, with him in the cabin.

Turkish Airlines

Book this
BLR / BOM / DEL → IST → TBS · one ticket, one airline

The only clean way to get all three of you to Tbilisi with Sheldon in the cabin, under the seat in front of you — not in the hold.

  • 8 kg limit including the carrier. A toy poodle plus a soft carrier lands around 4–5 kg, so Sheldon has comfortable margin.
  • Economy only. Pets aren't permitted in Business — so don't upgrade yourselves onto a flight he can't board.

Geopolitical stability — an honest read

You asked because there's a war running in the Middle East. The short answer for Georgia: the war doesn't reach you on the ground — it reaches you in the flight routing. The risks that actually matter here are domestic and Russian, not Middle Eastern.

What this means in practice

  • The Middle East war: Georgia is not a participant and shares no border with any combatant. The real-world effect on you is aviation — Gulf hub closures and reroutes. Routing via Istanbul rather than Doha or Dubai sidesteps most of it, which is the other reason section 03 lands on Turkish.
  • Tbilisi protests: frequent, sometimes large, and concentrated on Rustaveli Avenue around Parliament, mostly in the evening. Police have used tear gas and water cannon. They have not spread to Kazbegi, Kakheti or the tourist areas. Walk the other way, and don't take Sheldon anywhere near a crowd.
  • Abkhazia & South Ossetia: Russian-occupied, closed, and non-negotiable — do not travel there, and don't let a driver improvise a route near the administrative boundary lines. Nothing in your itinerary goes near them.
  • Official read: the US State Department still rates Georgia Level 1 — exercise normal precautions, its safest tier. The 2026 Global Peace Index places it around 94th of 163 — middling globally, dragged down by the frozen conflict rather than by street crime.
  • Insurance: mandatory from 1 January 2026 anyway (section 02) — buy a policy that includes trip disruption, so a rerouted or cancelled connection is covered.
How to read this score: it's our composite judgement across the five factors above — weighted for two tourists and a small dog on a two-week trip, not for a business investing there. It isn't an official index. Re-check the picture about a month before you fly.

What to cover — quaint, not commercial

Base twice · 3 nights, then 2

Old Tbilisi

Carved wooden balconies under the Narikala fortress; the domed sulphur bathhouses of Abanotubani; natural-wine bars; the Dry Bridge flea market; the Fabrika courtyard. Walkable, dog-tolerant, cheap.

Day trip · 40 min, easy road

Mtskheta

Ancient spiritual capital, UNESCO-listed: hilltop Jvari Monastery above two rivers, and 11th-century Svetitskhoveli cathedral. Quiet, sacred, an easy half-day from the Tbilisi base.

Base · 2 nights · the drama

Georgian Military Road → Kazbegi

~2.5–3hr drive in — Ananuri fortress on its reservoir, the Gudauri viewpoint, then Stepantsminda where the Gergeti Trinity Church stands alone against 5,047 m Mt Kazbek. In snow, the defining image of Georgia. The Truso Valley walk here is the trip's medium hike.

Base · 3 nights · wine + a fairytale town

Kakheti & Sighnaghi

Walled hilltop "city of love," terracotta roofs, Caucasus views. Family qvevri wineries pour amber wine from buried clay; Bodbe convent among cypresses; a day trip out to Telavi & Gremi. Slow food, warm hosts, no crowds.

A calm 11-day shape, no one-night stands: Tbilisi (3, base for the Mtskheta day trip) → Kazbegi (2) → Kakheti/Sighnaghi (3, base for wineries, Bodbe & a Telavi/Gremi day trip) → Tbilisi (2, birthday). Every stop gets at least two nights; nowhere do you pack a bag the morning after you unpacked it. All low-altitude, driveable, Sheldon-friendly. Got an extra day or two? Borjomi's forest trails and the Uplistsikhe cave town both work as an add-on base near the end, but neither is worth a one-night stop on their own.

Driving & car rental

Georgia is genuinely easy and rewarding to self-drive — right-hand traffic, signs in Georgian and English on main routes, airport pickups, and Google Maps works fine. The catch is assertive local driving and snowy mountain roads to Kazbegi/Gudauri in late November — so you want winter tyres and nerve, or a driver for that leg.

The honest self-drive verdict

  • City & wine country (Kakheti, Mtskheta): easy, flat, well-signed — great self-drive with Sheldon in his carrier or a harness.
  • Georgian Military Road to Kazbegi: spectacular but a real mountain road; in snow it demands winter tyres and confidence. Many couples take a private driver for this one day — the alternative, if you'd rather keep driving yourselves, is booking a 4x4/SUV with winter tyres already fitted, priced into the rental rather than hunted down at a Gudauri roadside stall.
  • Paperwork: carry an International Driving Permit (IDP) with your licence. Book online 2–3 days ahead — walk-up airport rates run 30–50% higher.
  • Insurance: third-party is included; add CDW/full cover (~₾30–60/day) for mountains. Deposit ~$180–360 blocked on a credit card.

Georgia + Armenia

საქართველო · Հայաստան — two countries, one road between them
The easy two-country trip. Eight days of Georgian mountains and wine, a four-hour drive south over the friendliest border in the Caucasus, then seven days of Armenian monasteries with Ararat on the skyline — and Sheldon crosses in the car, never needing a second seat on a plane.

01

Why this combination works

Of every pairing within reach of a fixed late-November date, this is the one that asks the least of you and the least of Sheldon. The reason is simple and slightly unglamorous: you can drive between them.

Border crossing4-hour drive
Extra flights for SheldonNone
Rabies titre testNot required
The split8 days + 7 days
  • One border, one road, no second aeroplane. Sheldon rides across the Sadakhlo–Bagratashen crossing on someone's lap. Every other pairing nearby — Azerbaijan, Turkey, anything in Central Asia — needs him booked into a second airline's cabin, with a second set of slots to compete for and a second chance to be told no at the gate.
  • Armenia asks less of a dog than anywhere else in the region. A microchip, a current rabies shot, and a vet health certificate issued in Georgia. No titre test, no 90-day wait, no import permit for one or two pets, no quarantine. Turkey — the country you first asked about — needs a rabies titre and a 90-day wait from the blood draw, which simply does not fit fixed November dates.
  • It costs you no extra flying. Fly Turkish into Tbilisi, leave Turkish out of Yerevan. An open-jaw on one ticket, one airline, Sheldon in the cabin both times, and not a single kilometre retraced.
  • They are far less alike than the map suggests. Georgia is Orthodox, wine-drunk and Black Sea–facing, its feasts built around a tamada and 8,000-year-old clay-buried qvevri. Armenia is the world's oldest Christian state, its churches carved from rose tuff and set in bare gorges, with Ararat — the mountain on its coat of arms, sitting just over a closed Turkish border — visible from half of Yerevan. Two weeks reads as two trips, not one long one.
  • Fifteen days divides cleanly. Eight days in Georgia, with Rachel's birthday on 23 November landing on day six in Tbilisi, where the sulphur baths and the best table in the country are. Then one travel day, then seven days in Armenia. Nothing is rushed and nothing repeats.
The honest trade-off: Armenia in late November is cold — Yerevan hovers near freezing, and the monasteries are colder still. You said the second country needn't be cold but should be memorable; this pairing buys ease and pays for it in temperature.

03

Crossing into Armenia with Sheldon

This is the part that makes the whole trip work, and it is genuinely undramatic. The Georgia–Armenia border is widely described as the easiest crossing in the Caucasus, and a dog changes that surprisingly little. The one thing you must not skip: the health certificate is issued in Georgia, not Armenia.

Step 1 · Tbilisi, day 5

Get a Georgian vet health certificate

Visit a Tbilisi vet on the afternoon you're back from Kazbegi, for a health certificate for non-commercial movement, dated within 10 days of travel — comfortably inside the window for crossing on day 9. This — not your Indian paperwork — is the document Armenian border vets actually read. Have Sheldon's chip number, rabies record and your passport with you.

book the vet the afternoon you're back from Kazbegi
Step 2 · Book the car

Two ways across — and you can keep the car

One-way rentals from Tbilisi to Yerevan do exist. Alamo, Enterprise, Europcar and FSTAR all run offices in both cities and will let you collect in Georgia and drop in Armenia; Enterprise says its rate includes the one-way fee and Armenian cover with no separate cross-border charge. It needs a cross-border authorisation (a power of attorney) arranged in advance — 48 hours' notice at the low end, a week at the high — and Georgian insurance does not extend into Armenia, so confirm the Armenian policy in writing. Published one-way fees are scarce; you have to ring and ask. The alternative is a private transfer at roughly $120–180, 4–5 hours, with a driver who crosses weekly. See section 13 for which one to pick.

arrange the authorisation a week ahead
Step 3 · The border

Sadakhlo – Bagratashen

The main Tbilisi–Yerevan road crossing. Expect to be asked to step out and open the doors with Sheldon restrained, then to park and bring the papers inside. Officers scan the microchip and check dates, stamps, signatures and that the owner's name matches your passport. Carry originals, not photocopies. Ten minutes on a good day, an hour if the vet officer is on the phone to a superior.

Step 4 · Armenia

What Armenia does not ask for

No rabies titre. No import permit for one or two pets travelling with their owner. No quarantine. This is the lightest pet-entry regime of anywhere you could reasonably reach on these dates.

Step 5 · Yerevan, day 13–14

An Armenian vet certificate for the flight home

The return leg leaves from Yerevan, so the fresh health certificate has to come from an Armenian vet, dated close to departure. Book it two days before you fly, not the morning of.

Step 6 · Return

Bringing Sheldon home to India

With the AQCS Advance NOC already filed under the Re-import category (Customs Circular 25/2013) using your original Bangalore export documents, he comes home without the 15-day quarantine that fresh imports face. This is why the originals matter.

What you are carrying across, in one line: microchip certificate · Indian rabies record · the AQCS export certificate and NOC (originals) · a Georgian vet health certificate under 10 days old · and later, an Armenian one for the way home.

Don't forget the Georgian insurance rule: since 1 January 2026 every visitor to Georgia must carry health and accident cover of at least 30,000 GEL (~$11,000). Buy one policy that covers both countries, the full 15 days, and includes trip disruption.

What this means in practice

  • The Middle East war: neither country is a participant. Armenia's short southern border with Iran is worth knowing about but has produced no spillover for travellers; the practical effect on you is still aviation, which the Istanbul routing largely handles.
  • Tbilisi protests: frequent, sometimes large, concentrated on Rustaveli Avenue by Parliament, usually after dark. They have not spread to Kazbegi or Kakheti. Walk away from crowds and don't take Sheldon near one.
  • Armenia's eastern frontier: the border with Azerbaijan has seen flare-ups and is not a place to wander. Everything in this itinerary — Yerevan, Geghard, Garni, Khor Virap, Noravank, Sevan, Dilijan, Haghpat — sits well clear of it. If you add Tatev in the far south, check advisories for Syunik first; the monastery itself is routinely visited.
  • Georgia's occupied regions: Abkhazia and South Ossetia are closed and non-negotiable. Nothing here goes near them.
  • Official read: the US State Department rates Georgia Level 1. Armenia carries a low-level advisory with a specific carve-out for the Azerbaijan border zone — which is exactly how to read it: fine country, one region to skip.
How to read these scores: our composite judgement across the five factors, weighted for two tourists and a small dog on a two-week trip. Not an official index. Re-check a month before you fly.

Base twice · 2 nights, then 2

Old Tbilisi

Carved wooden balconies under Narikala, the domed sulphur bathhouses of Abanotubani, natural-wine bars, the Dry Bridge flea market. Walkable, dog-tolerant and cheap — and the right place for a 40th dinner. The Mtskheta day trip (40 min, easy road) happens from the first stay.

Base · 2 nights · wine country

Kakheti & Sighnaghi

Half day · 40 min out, easy road

Mtskheta

The ancient spiritual capital, UNESCO-listed: hilltop Jvari Monastery above the meeting of two rivers, and the 11th-century Svetitskhoveli cathedral below.

09

Armenia — the seven days

Base · 2 nights, just over the border

Dilijan

Armenia's forested spa town, wooden balconies in a wooded valley — "little Switzerland" — and the natural place to break the border-crossing day rather than push straight to Yerevan. Haghpat & Sanahin are on the way in; the short walk to Parz Lake is the leg's small hike.

Base · 4 nights

Yerevan, the pink city

Built almost entirely from rose-and-apricot volcanic tuff, and older than Rome. The Cascade's stone staircase of sculpture terraces, Republic Square's singing fountains, the Vernissage flea market for silver and Soviet cameras, and a café culture that takes coffee seriously. Ararat closes the end of half the streets.

Day trip · 45 min out, easy road

Geghard & Garni

Geghard is half-carved directly into the cliff — chambers hewn from living rock, with a spring inside and acoustics that will stop you talking. Twenty minutes away, Garni is a Greco-Roman colonnaded temple on a gorge edge. Below it, the short scramble into Garni Gorge to the "Symphony of Stones" basalt columns is an optional bonus hike if legs are willing.

Day trip · the icon

Khor Virap, Noravank & Areni

Khor Virap sits alone on the plain with Ararat filling the sky behind it — the picture of Armenia. Then the red-rock gorge to Noravank, and Areni, where a cave held the world's oldest known winery at 6,100 years. The wine is still being made a few hundred metres away.

Worth knowing about Tatev: the cliff-top monastery reached by the world's longest reversible cable car is spectacular — and roughly four hours south of Yerevan each way, into Syunik. It works as an overnight, not a day trip. Left out of the plan below on purpose; swap it in for the slow Yerevan day if you'd rather have the drama.

The national barbecue — pork or lamb over vine cuttings, with grilled vegetables smoked alongside.

Vine leaves rolled around rice and herbs; the meatless pasuts version is the vegetarian hero.

Sweet layered pastry sold at every monastery gate — buy it warm at Geghard.

Indigenous Areni noir from the oldest winemaking region on earth — and the brandy Churchill is said to have kept ordering.

Getting around

Self-drive in Georgia, then a straight two-option call for the leg south: keep the Georgian car and cross it yourself, or let it go back to Tbilisi and pick up a fresh rental once you're through. One is clearly the easier trip for you three.

The honest verdict

  • Georgia, days 1–8: a compact self-drive is genuinely pleasant for Tbilisi, Mtskheta and Kakheti — flat, well-signed, and Sheldon rides with you. For the two Kazbegi transfer days (3 and 5), either take a private driver or, if you'd rather stay behind the wheel, book a 4x4/SUV with winter tyres — the cost of snow-readiness is baked into the rate rather than improvised roadside.
  • The border, day 9 — Best vs second best. Best: hire a private transfer, $120–180. No Power of Attorney, no cross-border insurance chase, no unpublished one-way fee — the driver crosses at Sadakhlo–Bagratashen every week and knows the vet window cold. You don't give up the self-drive days for it: pick up a fresh rental once you're in Dilijan or Yerevan and drive yourselves for the rest of Armenia instead. Second best: keep the Georgian car and cross it yourself on a one-way booking. It buys you days 9–10 at your own pace through the Debed canyon and Dilijan, but tack on $200+ for the cross-border authorisation and drop fee, book the Power of Attorney 48 hours to a week out, and get the Armenian insurance confirmed in writing — Georgian cover stops dead at the border. It also means your first stretch of Armenian driving is the Debed canyon, which has the roughest roads in the country.
  • What tips it, for you three: the driver's real value was never the driving — it was the border and the vet check, done by someone who's done it a hundred times. Take the transfer, then rent again once you're through: the same self-drive days in Armenia, none of the cross-border fees or paperwork, and no rough-road introduction to driving there.
  • Dilijan, days 9–10: you won't have a car yet if you took the transfer. Either extend the private-transfer driver into a short charter that covers the Parz Lake hop, or use a local taxi from the guesthouse — Dilijan is small enough that this is a five-minute ask, not a logistics problem.
  • Armenia, days 11–15: pick up a self-drive rental in Dilijan or Yerevan — roughly $20–35/day — for Geghard, Garni, Khor Virap, Noravank and Sevan at your own pace; the southern roads are in far better shape than the north. Yerevan itself is walkable, and taxis via Yandex Go or GG cover anything in the city. If nobody wants to drive, a hired car and driver at roughly $60–90/day is the fallback.
  • The cross-border paperwork: a power of attorney / cross-border authorisation from the rental company, requested anywhere from 48 hours to a week ahead. Crucially, Georgian insurance stops at the border — get the Armenian cover confirmed in writing, not verbally. Bring an International Driving Permit with your licence. One-way fees are rarely published; phone or WhatsApp the branch and get a number before you commit.
  • Crossing points: Sadakhlo–Bagratashen is the main one, 80 km south of Tbilisi and open 24 hours. Ninotsminda–Bavra is the quieter southern alternative.
